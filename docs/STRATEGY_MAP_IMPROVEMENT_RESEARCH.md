# Strategy-map improvement research

The existing strategy-map work already uses one composed atlas, deterministic
settlement marker spacing, and a gated mouse-wheel zoom. The remaining audit
found four display problems and they are now implemented with original code.

## Applied fixes

- [Uniform aspect-ratio scaling](<D:/AI-Related Apllications & Modding/Modding/Bannerlord Modding Stuff/Ages Of Calradia/CalendarWorldLedgerVM.cs:254>) fits the 1730x1720 source map to the viewport using one scale on both axes. The old independent X/Y scales stretched the map horizontally.
- [Selection feedback](<D:/AI-Related Apllications & Modding/Modding/Bannerlord Modding Stuff/Ages Of Calradia/GUI/Prefabs/WorldCalendar/WorldCalendar.xml:439>) now draws a layered gold glow around the clicked marker. The selection state drives both the glow layer and the crisp inner border.
- [Campaign borders](<D:/AI-Related Apllications & Modding/Modding/Bannerlord Modding Stuff/Ages Of Calradia/CampaignKingdomBorderBehavior.cs:1>) use independently calculated live-settlement Voronoi ownership, padded campaign-map bounds, terrain-height sampling, and `vertex_color_mat` runtime meshes. Research was limited to publicly documented behavior and screenshots; no third-party DLL was decompiled or used as an implementation source.
- The campaign renderer is fully self-contained and does not depend on or delegate rendering to Artem, Kingdom Frontiers, or Political Map. Disable other campaign-border features while testing to avoid duplicate meshes.
- Province-border ribbons and political terrain rows emit both triangle windings. This avoids relying on an undocumented campaign-material culling convention; small row batches keep the extra submissions bounded.
- The political layer keeps every ownership junction fixed to the live settlement Voronoi topology. An earlier two-sided faction-blend ribbon was removed after runtime screenshots showed translucent-looking bands. The newly requested reference treatment is implemented independently as one opaque pale outline sampled from final faction identity at four times the base-grid resolution. It suppresses boundaries between same-realm settlement cells, includes land/sea transitions so the outline follows the recovered coastline, and cannot create the old straight Voronoi chords through open water. A separate campaign-map layer densely samples internal ID changes from `strategic_province_index.png`, inverse-fits the authored map against live town/castle coordinates, terrain-projects the result, and renders opaque-black authored province contours.
- At distant campaign-map camera altitudes, the bounded grid samples the live Voronoi owner at each triangle center. Cached grid-vertex height queries drape the tint over mountain slopes and river terrain. Original kingdom labels use each realm's live town/castle centroid, Bannerlord's world-to-screen projection, and screen-space collision filtering. Native settlement names are suppressed only in this distant view and are restored automatically when zooming in; party markers remain independent.
- Authored province contours are a separate opaque-black layer above the political fill and pale faction frontier. A two-pass nearest-label transform recovers province IDs across the atlas's thick black separators. A marching-grid pass then joins edge crossings within each six-pixel cell, including explicit multi-province junctions; this replaces the rejected independent 22-pixel tangent strokes that produced random dashes. Ribbons are finalized in bounded 48-segment entities under a 4 ms map-frame budget, so construction continues while campaign time is paused. The earlier 6.5-unit live-frontier suppression was removed after runtime evidence showed it erased otherwise valid border sections and left large gaps; province contours may cross the separate faction outline without being deleted.
- Both province-border presentations are forced to absolute black: the campaign mesh uses opaque `#000000`, and the Strategic Map applies an opaque-black UI tint to the authored transparent contour sprite so colored source antialias pixels cannot leak through.
- The authored province network fades and hides through the same reversible entity-visibility path as the political layer, preventing close-zoom geometry leaks. Its connected 2.6-unit pure-black contour sits at a terrain-relative height of 5.5, just above the faction fill at 4.0, and pending batches remain hidden until an atomic swap. The former +12/+14.4 placement was rejected after runtime evidence showed faction-coloured fragments depth-cutting through storm smoke.
- Political meshes use an explicit late terrain-pass render order (`100` for faction fill and river caps, `110` for province contours), and fill entities do not force native terrain decals. This covers native roads, rivers, and terrain detail while storms, smoke, and weather remain independent atmosphere or particle visuals above the close-draped geometry.
- Distant political territory uses the recovered original 96-column base grid with two levels of refinement only across land/coast or ownership transitions. The earlier adaptive failure came from finalizing all 52,814 refined triangles in one giant mesh; row batching later proved that mesh finalization architecture, not refinement, caused the stall. The renderer now submits at most eight rows within a 4 ms visible-map-frame budget, including while campaign time is paused, initializes each row once with `SetReadyToRender(true)`, keeps pending rows hidden with the reversible `SetVisibilityExcludeParents(false)`, and atomically reveals the finished set. Active entities use the same visibility API below the political zoom threshold because the opaque campaign material does not reliably hide geometry through entity alpha alone. Kingdom labels remain hidden until the swap. Terrain sampling remains limited to a 5 ms/frame budget. Coastline classification projects the module-owned `strategic_province_index.png` through the settlement-calibrated campaign transform. A robust median/MAD ceiling derived from perimeter-water terrain heights distinguishes low open sea from elevated inland channels; only elevated gaps with opposing authored land banks are recovered. The same classification controls province-line coast clipping. A balanced spatial nearest-site index assigns live ownership. This is an original implementation using no third-party code or assets.
- Two earlier river classifiers were rejected from evidence. A wide opposite-bank-only preview visibly leaked into coastal bays, while a face-by-face navigation implementation measured 306,932 probes and a 53.3-second main-thread stall. Runtime evidence from the direct classifier then showed only 12 additional cells, so generic `Water` cannot be accepted blindly or rejected outright. The corrected path protects `CoastalSea`, `OpenSea`, and `SeaRestriction`, accepts native `River`, `Lake`, and land, and recovers generic-water gaps from their elevation above the measured sea baseline. Highly elevated interior gaps no longer require a perfectly straight opposing-bank ray; lower channels still do. Native terrain-category counts are logged for the next runtime audit.
- The current 96-column base cache samples each grid vertex once. Bilinear heights remain the bounded base surface, while adaptive relief vertices and province contours use the scene's exact local terrain-height call. Fine political land classification first accepts authored land without a native terrain-type call, then queries native terrain only for mask gaps such as rivers and omitted settlement-bearing peninsulas. This avoids the rejected navigation-face search. Diagnostics report cache, exact-height, native terrain-query, refined-cell, total mesh, maximum per-batch, and sea-height classification values, while an ownership signature prevents unconditional daily rebuilds.
- Native v1.4.7 inspection established that `CampaignVec2.IsValid()` combines face existence with party-navigation eligibility. It is therefore unsuitable for terrain classification because valid `Water` or `Lake` faces can be rejected before their terrain type is read. The political cache now tests `CampaignVec2.Face.IsValid()` instead. Refined authored-land samples retain the fast path except inside coarse cells already identified as protected water; only that narrow coast band receives an exact native recheck. This closes adaptive coastline slivers without restoring the rejected all-point navigation scan. Runtime diagnostics report exact native probes and protected-water rejections separately.
- The post-deployment log still found 81 native `River` cells while the user observed uncovered rivers, proving ownership classification alone was not the remaining defect. A native-water-height experiment then sampled 2,710 river triangles but lifted none: the existing terrain-relative vertices were already above the reported water surface. This rejected depth as the cause and showed that the river presentation renders later. River-classified triangles form an isolated cap using the same original opaque faction material and render order `100` as ordinary land. Neither cap nor land forces decals; province contours render immediately after at `110` so the black network remains continuous. The cap retains the same close terrain height and does not create a globally elevated sheet through weather.
- The next deployed cap produced 1,590 river triangles in 64 dedicated entities, but the screenshot remained unchanged and showed native roads, rivers, and terrain detail instead of solid-black province contours. That evidence rejected render order alone. The actual visual leak was the combination of `vertex_color_blend_mat` and forced decals, both of which intentionally preserve native map detail. The accepted clean presentation uses opaque `vertex_color_mat` for both ordinary land and river caps at render order `100`, disables forced terrain decals on both, and draws the opaque-black province network at `110`. Storms, smoke, and weather remain separate particle/atmosphere visuals above the close-draped geometry; native road and river appearance no longer substitutes for province borders.
- The first opaque-material screenshot exposed scattered black mountain spots while almost all intended province contours disappeared. Their matching appearance strongly indicates one shared depth-intersection defect: the 96-column bilinear surface cuts through sharp terrain peaks, and exact authored contour points can sit below that surface. The corrected drape keeps the bounded base grid but compares each candidate cell's interpolated center against an exact native terrain-height sample; cells with at least `0.75` units of error refine through the existing two-level limit, and all added vertices use exact local heights. Province ribbon endpoints now use the same exact-height path. Modest offsets of `4.0` for fill and `5.5` for contours preserve the required layer separation without returning to the rejected `+12/+14.4` geometry that pierced storm smoke. Exact-height sample count, native-call time, and relief-refined cell count are logged for runtime performance auditing.
- The close campaign view now retains only the opaque pale live-faction frontier over native terrain. Frontier entities are separated from territory-fill entities at the atomic builder handoff: the fill and black authored province network continue to use the distant political-zoom alpha, while faction outlines stay at full opacity at normal and close zoom. This avoids tinting or replacing local terrain, roads, rivers, settlement labels, and party presentation in the detailed campaign view.
- Close-zoom runtime feedback showed that reusing the political frontier's `+5f` height made the outline sit visibly above detailed terrain. The first correction tested a zoom-dependent `-3.5f` frame offset for a `+1.5f` close drape while retaining `+5f` above the opaque distant fill. This established that one shared coast-following mesh can move smoothly with political alpha without a duplicate frontier layer; the tighter final drape is recorded below.
- Further visual direction called for a thinner, more grounded, two-sided shared frontier. The independent renderer uses one `2.4f` ribbon split exactly along its centerline. It samples live faction identity on the left and right of each segment and darkens each side's faction colour to 65% brightness; a coast segment uses the land faction on both halves. This is one mesh line rather than overlapping border entities. Exact terrain heights are sampled for the centerline and both outer edges. An intermediate close drape of `+0.75f` was still visibly higher than the reference. The style target came from the supplied screenshot only; no third-party code, IL, constants, shaders, meshes, or assets were inspected or copied.
- Direct comparison with the supplied reference showed its close border reads as decal-flat rather than merely low. The final close frame drop is therefore `4.8f`, placing the exact-width-sampled ribbon at `+0.2f` above terrain. That small residual margin avoids coplanar z-fighting while preserving the near-ground appearance; political zoom still transitions to `+5f` above the opaque fill.
- Close-zoom feedback then identified visible angularity in the four-times marching contour. Frontier sampling is now eight times the 96-column base grid, halving each contour cell's world-space span and producing a materially smoother coast and faction curve without changing ownership topology. The left/right faction probe contracts from `2.75f` to `1.5f` so it remains local to the finer cell. Construction still advances under the existing eight-row/four-millisecond frame budget, trading a longer hidden preparation for bounded map-frame work rather than a synchronous stall.
- The first eight-times runtime screenshot proved resolution was not sufficient by itself: individual rectangular segment ends separated at turns and overlapped into pointed wedges. The connected centerline was correct, but its independently offset square-ended ribbons had no join geometry. Each segment is now emitted as a split-colour capsule. Four terrain-projected steps form each left and right semicircle at both endpoints, overlapping adjacent segments with rounded joins while preserving the faction colour on each side. This addresses the confirmed geometry discontinuity without blurring or moving the live ownership contour.
- Runtime diagnostics after rounded caps showed the eight-times build produced 11,766 segments, 781 frontier entities, 539,844 exact-height samples, 1,311,235 exact terrain probes, 12.9 seconds of measured mesh work, and a 145 ms maximum batch. The completed diagnostic appeared roughly 28 seconds after map-state creation. Because fill and frontier were held for one atomic swap, both presentations appeared absent during that delay. The accepted recovery returns frontier sampling to four-times while retaining rounded joins, and splits publication into two atomic phases: faction fill becomes visible immediately after its rows finish, while the old frontier remains until the replacement frontier is complete. This preserves visual continuity and removes the eight-times workload that did not solve the actual square-join defect.
- The same report also identified excessive width and flashing. Flashing had two independent depth causes: every adjoining segment emitted another coplanar round cap at the same endpoint, and the close ribbon sat only `0.2f` above terrain. A quantized endpoint set now permits exactly one cap per shared point across all row entities, width is reduced from `2.4f` to `1.6f`, and close drape is raised minimally to `+0.35f`. This removes overlapping join geometry and gives terrain depth a small stable margin while remaining visually ground-hugging.
- Political colour balance now separates area readability from frontier contrast. Territory colours preserve the original faction hue and channel relationships, scale uniformly to 50%, and render at full distant opacity. This matches the flat solid-colour reference while making every country darker. Each frontier half still uses the original 85% hue-preserving scale and remains deliberately brighter than the territory field.
- Close-zoom province borders reuse the already extracted Strategic Map contour entities instead of doubling them. The first `1.0f`/`+0.4f` presentation built all 5,452 segments and 114 entities successfully according to runtime diagnostics but remained visually buried by close terrain. The shared black ribbon is now `1.4f` wide and its entity frame interpolates from `-4.7f` at close zoom (net terrain drape `+0.8f`) to identity at political zoom (`+5.5f`). The frame is applied only when political alpha changes by more than `0.002`; there is no second contour pass, second terrain projection, duplicate entity set, or steady-state frame mutation.
- Political fill presentation reaches 100% opacity while its faction RGB is scaled uniformly to 50%. Earlier partial-opacity variants looked washed out because bright terrain diluted the hue. A proposed saturation enhancement was rejected after the visual reference clarified that the desired treatment preserves the original flat faction hues and only darkens them. Live country frontiers remain fully opaque for readability.
- Campaign province contours were subsequently removed at the user's request. Their behavior is no longer registered or compiled, eliminating contour extraction, 114 render entities, and zoom-frame updates. This does not remove or alter the Strategic Map's authored province-border artwork.
- A close-coast screenshot exposed a frontier chord connecting separate land pieces across water. The cause was the generic four-crossing junction path: an alternating land/water marching cell emitted four spokes through its center. Alternating saddle cells now use center-classified adjacent-edge pairing, and every candidate segment must retain land support on at least one side at five points along its length. Unsupported open-water chords are discarded without removing valid shoreline contours.
- A wider archipelago screenshot showed that separate islands could still be merged before contour extraction. The narrow opposing-bank heuristic was classifying low sea channels like inland rivers. Strict atlas land is now tracked separately; only strict pixels bypass elevation checks. Recovered gaps receive exact native terrain classification and a sea-ceiling test, preserving true land and rivers while leaving low channels between islands disconnected.
- Follow-up screenshots showed that fill recovery and frontier topology need different generic-water policy. The fill retains elevation-aware generic-water recovery to cover authored river gaps. The frontier classifier instead rejects native generic `Water` outside strict authored land while accepting explicit native `River` and land terrain. This keeps every island coastline as an independent loop without regressing river coverage beneath the country tint.
- Enclosed native `Lake` terrain is political interior terrain rather than protected sea. It inherits nearest-settlement ownership for solid country tint and does not generate an internal coast frontier. `CoastalSea`, `OpenSea`, `SeaRestriction`, and generic-water archipelago channels retain their exclusion rules.
- A remaining Battania lake hole showed that its native classification could still be generic `Water`. The strategic index now computes connected exterior water once with an edge-seeded flood fill. Any authored water component not connected to an atlas edge is an enclosed lake and is political interior terrain; channels around islands are edge-connected and remain sea. This map-topology rule is independent of native water labels.
- The index's black province separators initially made the lake appear edge-connected. The topology flood fill now treats only opposing-land gaps within four pixels as solid separator recovery. This seals divider strokes but cannot join the much wider real-water gaps between islands.
- A subsequent reference comparison showed that the reliable authority must be campaign terrain rather than the authored province atlas. The independent implementation now performs a second connected-component pass over its already sampled native terrain grid. Any water component reaching a grid edge remains sea; an enclosed component receives political fill. This is bounded by the existing 96-column cache and does not introduce per-point navigation scans.
- The Strategic Map also rebuilds gold kingdom names from live ownership. Each name is centered on the largest spatially connected cluster of that kingdom's authored-map settlement anchors, with deterministic overlap displacement; therefore conquests move the country name during the same ownership-revision refresh that recolours provinces.
- Friendly armies and player-owned parties are a separate lightweight Strategic Map layer. It includes every active friendly/formally allied army leader, the player's main party, and every active player-clan party, with one marker per party even when categories overlap. `P`, `C`, and `A` glyphs distinguish those roles. All use the same independently calibrated campaign-to-authored-map projection and refresh once per second without recomposing the province texture.
- The Strategic Map legend repeats those exact live-marker glyphs and colors as `P Player party`, `C Clan party`, and `A Friendly army`. Its reserved height and separator are layout-bound, and the summary viewport contracts by the same expansion so legend rows never overlap kingdom or settlement information.
- Strategic kingdom lettering uses one gold text widget. The offset shadow copy was removed because at full-map zoom it visibly doubled every country name.
- Campaign-map kingdom lettering is also standalone: the former translucent black backing rectangle was removed, leaving only the zoom-projected faction-coloured gold-style name text.
- [Collision-aware town labels](<D:/AI-Related Apllications & Modding/Modding/Bannerlord Modding Stuff/_TwelveMonthCalendar/CalendarStrategicMapTextureProvider.cs:797>) prioritizes shorter names and tries four deterministic positions. Labels that would overlap another label or leave the map are skipped instead of producing unreadable text piles.

The composed texture remains the source of territory, settlement symbols, and
siege badges. The marker buttons remain hit targets only; they do not alter
settlement positions. The static province border artwork is enabled above the
composed strategic-map texture, while the selected marker glow is drawn by
the live Gauntlet marker layer.

## Deferred intentionally

The province ownership index and static owner bindings need a separate asset
audit before changing. The current renderer uses that index only as an authored
land/coast mask and province contour source; live settlement ownership still
determines faction colour. Changing the source index would risk miscoloring the
133 audited regions.
